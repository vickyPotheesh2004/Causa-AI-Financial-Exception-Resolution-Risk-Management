Absolutely. This is the **third major perspective** we should finalize: the **Department Side**.

The employee side answers **“What is happening and what do I need to do?”**  
The department side answers **“Which cases have been assigned to us, what evidence do we have, what decision/action is required, what has already been done, and what remains unresolved?”**

The department interface should therefore be a **case-resolution workspace**, not simply another dashboard.

> **Important architecture point:** your system should not hard-code or claim knowledge of Razorpay's internal organizational structure. Instead, define **configurable department queues** such as Settlement Operations, Refund Operations, Risk/Fraud, Compliance, Support, etc. The routing engine assigns a case according to configured issue type, risk, authority, and policy.

# 2. What the Department Receives

When the AI escalates an issue, the department should **not receive a blank ticket saying**:

> “Please investigate settlement issue.”

That would simply move the manual work from one employee to another.

Instead, the department receives a **fully prepared investigation package**.

For example:

```text
TICKET: RZ-INC-82931

Issue:
Settlement discrepancy

Merchant:
ABC Technologies Pvt Ltd

Exception:
EXC-2026-000184

Financial impact:
₹250 unexplained discrepancy

Risk:

DEPARTMENT_ESCALATE

Reason:
Authoritative records do not currently explain
the remaining discrepancy.

Evidence:
EV-001
EV-002
EV-003

Rules checked:
RZ-001
RZ-002

Investigation:
Completed

AI recommendation:
Review settlement adjustment records.

Action already performed by AI:
NONE
```

So the department starts with **context**, not from zero.

# 3. Department Types

I recommend configuring departments around **case ownership**, rather than assuming a particular real-world organizational chart.

Example:

### Settlement Operations

Handles:

```text
Settlement mismatch
Missing settlement
Settlement timing exception
Settlement adjustment
Bank-to-settlement discrepancy
```

### Refund Operations

Handles:

```text
Refund discrepancy
Refund pending
Refund status conflict
Refund-related exceptions
```

### Risk / Fraud

Handles:

```text
Possible fraud
Suspicious activity
Repeated anomalous patterns
High-risk financial cases
```

### Compliance

Handles:

```text
Policy conflict
Regulatory uncertainty
Required evidence issues
Compliance-sensitive cases
```

### Support / Merchant Operations

Handles:

```text
Merchant-reported issues
Repeated merchant complaints
Operational issues
Cases requiring merchant communication
```

### Technical / Platform Operations

Handles:

```text
API/data inconsistency
System-generated mismatch
Webhook/data synchronization issue
Technical processing failure
```

These are **configurable queues**.

# 44. The most important department-side principle

The department should **never receive just a problem**.

It should receive:

> **Problem + Context + Investigation + Evidence + Sources + Applicable Rules + Risk + AI Decision + Required Action.**

And when the department finishes:

> **Department Finding + New Evidence + Resolution + Verification**

gets added back to the same case.

## The key difference

### Employee side

**"What happened and what do I need to know/do?"**

### AI system

**"What evidence, rules, risk and authority determine what should happen?"**

### Department side

**"How do we investigate, act, resolve and formally close the case?"**

That separation is important because it prevents the application from becoming a generic "AI dashboard." It becomes a genuine **financial operations workflow system** where the AI prepares the case, employees understand it, and authorized departments resolve it—with every step connected through the same evidence-backed case record.
